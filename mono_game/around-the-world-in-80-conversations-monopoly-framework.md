# Around the World in 80 Conversations
## The Intercultural Communication Monopoly — Content Framework (Revised)
**Full English Edition · CEFR B1–B2 · 10 Zones × 8 Cards = 80 Conversations**

---

## 1. Game Concept

Players travel around the world board as international students and young professionals. Every space belongs to one of **10 Intercultural Zones**. When you land on a zone space, you draw a **Conversation Card** and complete a real communication task in English. Success earns you the space (plant your flag) and **Connection Points (CP)** — the game's currency. The winner is not the richest player, but the **best-connected communicator**.

**Tagline:** *10 Zones. 8 Conversations in Each Zone. 80 Chances to Connect.*

---

## 2. The Board (40 Spaces, Monopoly Layout)

| Space Type | Count | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Zone spaces (10 zones × 3) | 30 | Draw a Conversation Card from that zone's deck |
| Corner spaces | 4 | Departure Gate · Layover Lounge · Culture Shock! · World Forum |
| Passport Cards (Chance) | 3 | Random travel events |
| Global Community Cards | 3 | Cooperative / reflection events |

### The Four Corners

| Corner | Monopoly Equivalent | Rule (student-facing English) |
|---|---|---|
| **Departure Gate** | GO | "Collect 200 CP every time you pass. Your journey continues!" |
| **Layover Lounge** | Free Parking | "Take a rest. Ask any player one question about their culture." |
| **Culture Shock!** | Go to Jail | "Oh no — a big misunderstanding! Go directly to Lost in Translation." |
| **Lost in Translation** | Jail | "You are stuck! To get out, complete a Repair card from any zone — or wait one turn." |

> **Pedagogical note:** The jail mechanic is the heart of the game's message — *the only way out of a communication breakdown is repair, not avoidance.*

### Zone Space Tiers (rent = task difficulty)

Each zone has 3 spaces of increasing value. The tier decides which card type you draw:

| Tier | Space Cost | Card Type Drawn | Cognitive Demand |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Low (60–100 CP) | **Interpret** | Notice and explain |
| Tier 2 | Mid (120–200 CP) | **Respond** | Speak appropriately |
| Tier 3 | High (220–350 CP) | **Negotiate / Repair** | Solve a real problem |

Landing on another player's flagged space? You don't pay rent — you complete a **mini-challenge**: the owner reads you a follow-up question from their card. Answer well and pay half; struggle and pay full. (Communication is always cheaper than silence.)

## 3. The 10 Intercultural Zones (Revised, All-English)

Each zone now includes: a board color, a student-facing tagline (B1 level), core topics, and a CEFR can-do statement.

### Zone 1 · Greeting Gate 🟤 (Brown)
- **Tagline:** *First meetings, first impressions.*
- **Topics:** names and titles, handshakes and bows, eye contact, personal space, small talk, introducing yourself
- **Can-do (B1):** I can introduce myself politely and notice how greetings are different across cultures.

### Zone 2 · Language Lane 🔵 (Light Blue)
- **Tagline:** *It's not just what you say — it's how you say it.*
- **Topics:** direct vs. indirect language, silence, humor, accents, idioms, asking for clarification
- **Can-do (B1+):** I can ask someone to explain what they mean without making them feel uncomfortable.

### Zone 3 · Classroom Crossing 🩷 (Pink)
- **Tagline:** *Every classroom has its own culture.*
- **Topics:** teacher–student relationships, class participation, asking questions, giving feedback, group work norms
- **Can-do (B1+):** I can participate in an international classroom and explain my learning habits to others.

### Zone 4 · Teamwork Town 🟠 (Orange)
- **Tagline:** *Different teammates, one goal.*
- **Topics:** leadership styles, dividing work, time and deadlines, decision-making, responsibility, disagreement in teams
- **Can-do (B2):** I can discuss how to share work fairly in a multicultural team and suggest a plan everyone accepts.

### Zone 5 · Food & Festival Avenue 🔴 (Red)
- **Tagline:** *Sharing food, sharing culture.*
- **Topics:** food rules and restrictions, giving gifts, festivals, visiting someone's home, inviting and paying the bill
- **Can-do (B1):** I can politely accept or refuse food and explain my own food culture.

### Zone 6 · Digital Culture District 🟡 (Yellow)
- **Tagline:** *Culture doesn't disappear online — it gets faster.*
- **Topics:** reply speed, emojis and stickers, social media norms, online meetings, cameras on/off, privacy
- **Can-do (B1+):** I can communicate politely in messages and online meetings with people from other cultures.

### Zone 7 · Workplace World 🟢 (Green)
- **Tagline:** *Getting the job done — together.*
- **Topics:** power distance, meeting culture, deadlines, giving and receiving feedback, workplace relationships, after-work socializing
- **Can-do (B2):** I can handle common workplace situations, such as disagreeing with a manager politely.

### Zone 8 · Identity Island 🔷 (Dark Blue)
- **Tagline:** *You are more than one thing.*
- **Topics:** nationality, ethnicity, language, region, gender, generation, profession, multiple identities, belonging
- **Can-do (B2):** I can describe the different parts of my identity and avoid making quick judgments about others.
- **Design purpose:** Prevents students from reducing intercultural communication to "Taiwanese people vs. foreigners." Identity is layered — one person, many cultures.

### Zone 9 · Conflict & Repair Harbor ⚓ (Purple)
- **Tagline:** *Misunderstandings happen. Repair is a skill.*
- **Topics:** misunderstanding, apologizing, saving face, saying no, negotiating, rebuilding trust
- **Can-do (B2):** I can describe a problem without blaming, explain my intention, and suggest a way forward.
- **Design purpose:** Moves the game from *knowing about differences* to *fixing real communication problems*. This zone also powers the "Lost in Translation" escape mechanic.

### Zone 10 · Global Citizenship Square 🌍 (Board's "Boardwalk" pair)
- **Tagline:** *Think bigger than borders.*
- **Topics:** stereotypes, bias, microaggressions, cultural appropriation, inclusion, global responsibility
- **Can-do (B2):** I can recognize a stereotype, explain why it is a problem, and respond in a respectful way.

## 4. The Card System: 80 Conversations

### 4.1 Four Task Types (unchanged balance, revised English)

| Task Type | Cards per Zone | Total | Player's Job (student-facing) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Interpret** 🔍 | 2 | 20 | "What could this mean? Give TWO possible explanations before you judge." |
| **Respond** 💬 | 2 | 20 | "What would you say? Reply in polite, natural English." |
| **Negotiate** 🤝 | 2 | 20 | "Find a solution both sides can accept. Talk it through." |
| **Repair** 🔧 | 2 | 20 | "Something went wrong. Clarify, apologize if needed, and rebuild the conversation." |

### 4.2 The 8-Card Learning Cycle (revised order)

The original sequence (Notice → Interpret → Compare → Respond → Clarify → Negotiate → Repair → Reflect) mixed the task-type balance. The revised order keeps all 8 learning moves **and** maps cleanly onto the four task types:

| Card # | Learning Move | Task Type | What the player does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **Notice** | Interpret | Spot the cultural clue in a short scene |
| 02 | **Interpret** | Interpret | Offer 2+ possible explanations |
| 03 | **Respond** | Respond | Say something appropriate in the moment |
| 04 | **Clarify** | Respond | Ask a polite question to check meaning |
| 05 | **Compare** | Negotiate | Compare two ways of doing things, find common ground |
| 06 | **Negotiate** | Negotiate | Agree on a shared way forward |
| 07 | **Repair** | Repair | Fix a misunderstanding that already happened |
| 08 | **Reflect & Repair** | Repair | Connect to your own experience; plan what you'd do differently |

Each zone deck is shuffled in play, but the numbering preserves the cycle for classroom (non-board) use — a teacher can run any single zone as a complete 8-step lesson.

### 4.3 Card Numbering (unchanged)

GG01–GG08 · LL01–LL08 · CC01–CC08 · TT01–TT08 · FF01–FF08 · DC01–DC08 · WW01–WW08 · II01–II08 · CR01–CR08 · GC01–GC08

## 5. Unified Card Format (B1–B2)

Every card follows the same template — essential for the interactive version later:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [ZONE ICON]  GG03 · GREETING GATE           │
│ Task: RESPOND 💬          Level: B1          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ SITUATION (40–60 words, B1 vocabulary)      │
│ A short, concrete scene with named          │
│ characters from different cultures.         │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ YOUR TASK (one clear instruction)           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 🗣 PHRASE BANK (3 sentence starters)        │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ⭐ CHALLENGE (optional B2 follow-up)         │
│ 💰 REWARD: 100 CP (+50 CP for Challenge)    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Teacher's side (answer key) for each card:**
- 2–3 acceptable sample responses
- Cultural note (what research/common experience says — no single "correct culture" answer)
- Common B1 errors to listen for
- One extension question

### 5.1 Sample Card — GG03 (to fix the format)

> **GG03 · Greeting Gate · RESPOND 💬 · B1**
>
> **Situation:** You are at an international student welcome party. A new classmate from Brazil greets you with a hug and a kiss on the cheek. You feel surprised because in your culture, people usually just smile and nod. Your classmate notices your face and looks worried.
>
> **Your task:** Respond in a friendly way. Show that you are happy to meet them, and explain your surprise politely.
>
> **🗣 Phrase Bank:**
> - "Oh! Sorry, I was just surprised because in my culture, we usually..."
> - "It's really nice to meet you! In Taiwan, people often greet by..."
> - "Don't worry — I'd love to learn how people say hello in Brazil!"
>
> **⭐ Challenge (B2):** Your classmate asks, "So what should I do next time?" Suggest a greeting that works for both of you.
>
> **💰 Reward:** 100 CP (+50 CP for Challenge)

## 6. Special Card Decks (Monopoly Flavor)

### Passport Cards (Chance) — 12 cards, light and fun, B1 English
Examples:
- "Your flight is delayed 6 hours. You start chatting with the passenger next to you. Tell the group one interesting thing about your hometown. **Collect 50 CP.**"
- "You used the wrong emoji in a group chat and everyone is confused! **Go to Digital Culture District** and draw a card."
- "You gave a wonderful toast at an international dinner. **Advance to Food & Festival Avenue.**"
- "You laughed at a joke you didn't understand. **Pay 50 CP** — honesty is cheaper!"

### Global Community Cards — 12 cards, cooperative and reflective
Examples:
- "Class fund: Every player shares one English phrase they learned today. Everyone collects 20 CP."
- "A classmate is sitting alone at lunch. Say something to include them. **Collect 80 CP.**"
- "World Forum invitation: On your next turn, you may answer a Global Citizenship Square card for double points."

## 7. B1–B2 Language Calibration Rules (for writing all 80 cards)

1. **Situations:** 40–60 words, present tense preferred, concrete scenes, named characters (mix of nationalities — avoid "a foreigner").
2. **Vocabulary:** CEFR B1 core for situations; B2 words allowed in Challenge sections only, and glossed if abstract (e.g., *stereotype = a fixed, too-simple idea about a group*).
3. **Instructions:** One sentence, one verb, imperative form ("Explain...", "Suggest...", "Ask...").
4. **Phrase banks:** Always 3 starters, always incomplete (students must finish the sentence — output, not reading aloud).
5. **No single correct culture:** Interpret cards must accept multiple valid answers; teacher notes model *possibility language* ("Maybe... / It could be that... / One explanation is...").
6. **Repair language across all zones** draws on a shared toolkit: *describe, don't blame → explain intention → apologize or clarify → suggest a new way.*

---

## 8. Winning the Game

Play ends after a set time (e.g., 40 minutes) or when one player owns a full zone (all 3 spaces of one color).

**Final score = CP + Bonuses:**
- **Zone Master** (own all 3 spaces of a zone): +200 CP
- **Bridge Builder** (own at least one space in 5+ different zones): +300 CP — *rewarding breadth over monopoly, which is the intercultural point*
- **Repair Hero** (completed 3+ Repair cards): +150 CP

> Design intention: a player can win through *many small connections across zones* rather than dominating one — the scoring system itself teaches the course's core value.
